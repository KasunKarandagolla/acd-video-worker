# Football Emotion Pattern Library (shared reference)

Used by: football-story-strategy, football-source-discovery, football-clip-scoring,
football-narration-scriptwriting.

Source: consolidated from source pack file `03_football_emotion_pattern_library_source.md`.

## Core principle

Football emotion is created by `pressure → action → reaction → meaning`, not by goals alone.
A goal without pressure is a highlight. A missed penalty without reaction is trivia. A crying
face without context is exploitation. Every clip's job is to serve one link in that chain.

## Story structures (pick one per project, based on requested emotion — never default to comeback)

| Story type | Best for | Required footage minimum | 8–12 min timing skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain → Pressure → Comeback → Legacy | legacy completion, redemption | old failure, current pressure, turning point, release, legacy confirmation | 0:00–0:20 pain hook · 0:20–2:00 past failure/context · 2:00–5:00 pressure/doubt · 5:00–8:30 comeback/decisive · 8:30–12:00 legacy aftermath |
| Doubt → Proof → Celebration | iconic skill as proof, dominance | doubt/criticism, skill under pressure, opponent reaction, validation, ending | — |
| Final Chance → Failure → Emotional Ending | heartbreak, last-chance stories | setup, decisive failure, player reaction, crowd/team reaction, respectful close | — |
| Rivalry → Humiliation → Revenge/Answer | response/rematch stories | rivalry setup, humiliation, pressure, decisive answer, opponent/fan reaction | — |
| Nation Hopes → Player Burden → Decisive Moment | World Cup pressure, national hero | fans/flags, close-ups, match context, decisive action, national reaction | — |
| Heroic Failure | brilliant performance, still loses | team struggle, player response goals, repeated effort, final defeat, lonely aftermath | core line: "he did everything, but it was still not enough" |
| Shock Collapse | humiliation/collapse (restraint required) | expectation setup, first crack, rapid collapse, shocked faces, aftermath silence | fast during collapse, slow after damage lands |

## Hook patterns (pick one per project — the first 5–10 seconds must show/imply the emotional promise)

1. **Pain Before Glory** — loss/criticism/crying face first, 10–20s, darker tone, transition to hope via music shift.
2. **Final Moment First** — trophy/whistle/scoreboard/tunnel walk shown briefly, 5–12s, then hard-cut to earlier context.
3. **Commentator Shock Hook** — genuine scream/roar/famous line, 3–7s, audio before full visual reveal.
4. **Silent Emotional Face** — close-up, silence, 4–10s, hold longer than instinct says to.
5. **Quote/Text Hook** — one strong sentence, 3–6s, minimal words, no misleading claims.
6. **Scoreboard Shock** — instant stakes via score, 3–8s, must cut to a face/reaction immediately after.
7. **Skill-As-Proof Hook** — dribble/assist tied to doubt or pressure, 5–15s, not a compilation.
8. **Nation Burden Hook** — flags/fans/anthem/families, 8–20s, keep the individual player's story present too.

## Clip types ranked by emotional importance (use this to prioritize footage, not popularity)

1. Player face close-up
2. Immediate player reaction
3. Decisive action moment
4. Crowd/fan reaction
5. Commentator reaction/audio
6. Missed chance/penalty miss
7. Trophy lift/final whistle
8. Scoreboard/final result (must pair with a reaction shot)
9. Coach/team reaction
10. Opponent reaction
11. Slow-motion skill (proof, not filler)
12. Historical flashback (short, relevant only)
13. Fan crying (strong but easy to overuse)
14. Newspaper/social reaction (context only)
15. Generic celebration (low value unless tied to meaning)

## Retention patterns (apply throughout, not just at the hook)

- Open loop question early ("why did this moment hurt so much?")
- Emotional reversal every 90–150 seconds
- Never let action stand alone — pair with face/crowd/commentary reaction
- Flashbacks explain pressure, they don't interrupt momentum
- Silence before impact (penalty, final whistle, emotional face)
- A smaller micro-climax before the main climax
- End with meaning, not just the last goal

## The professional-vs-amateur test

Amateur: random goals first, no context, no arc, music does all the work, no reaction shots,
captions state the obvious, effects decorate instead of clarify.

Professional: human emotional promise first, efficient context, alternating action/reaction,
silence used as a tool, goals treated as story beats, an ending with meaning.

Ask, for every clip and every effect: **"What is the emotional job of this clip?"** If the answer
is unclear, it's probably filler and should be rejected or replaced.

## Instruction-to-pattern quick map

- "Messi proving everyone wrong" → Pain→Pressure→Comeback→Legacy + Pain Before Glory hook + Skill-As-Proof inserts. Avoid random club skills unrelated to World Cup pressure.
- "Ronaldo heartbreak" → Final Chance→Failure→Emotional Ending + Silent Emotional Face hook. Avoid mocking edits or unrelated rivalry clips.
- "World Cup pressure moment" → Nation Hopes→Player Burden→Decisive Moment + Commentator Shock or Silent Face hook. Avoid unclear-stakes clips.
- "Football comeback after humiliation" → Pain→Pressure→Comeback→Legacy + Scoreboard Shock hook. Requires a visible low point — no low point, no comeback story.
- "Player crying after final" → Final Moment First or Silent Emotional Face, aftermath-driven. Avoid crying-as-clickbait without context.
- "Iconic dribbling moment" → Skill-As-Proof + Doubt→Proof→Celebration. Avoid stacking many dribbles with no stakes.
- "Underdog team shocked the world" → Nation Hopes→Player Burden→Decisive Moment + crowd/atmosphere hook. Avoid showing only the winning goal — collective emotion is required.

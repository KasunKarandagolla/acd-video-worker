# Graphics, Text & Typography

Read this when placing text, title cards, quotes, or lower thirds (Stage 6–7, during visual assembly). This stage adds the metadata layer that guides viewers and reinforces the story.

## The text principle: minimum effective dose

Text on video is powerful but can also overwhelm. Use the least text that still clarifies the story.

**Text serves one of three functions; know which one:**
1. **Clarification** — the viewer doesn't know what they're seeing (e.g., "2015 — Before")
2. **Emphasis** — reinforcing the most important moment (e.g., the peak quote)
3. **Metadata** — credits, titles, context (less critical than 1 and 2)

Overuse typically happens with function 3 — every line of VO has a text card, every speaker is credited, etc. Usually you can cut 30–50% of this without hurting the piece.

## Text hierarchy by arc phase

**Cold open:** Title card only
- Large, bold, single line or two-line max
- 2–3 second hold
- Example: "The Greatest Comeback Nobody Expected" or just the player's name + year
- Not: a long explanation. That's not a hook.

**Setup/context:** Clarification text only (if needed)
- Small-to-medium size, brief (1–2 words or a short date)
- Example: "2015 — The Fall" or "Before his injury"
- Duration: 1–2 seconds or tie to the clip (text out when clip changes)
- Position: lower-third, not center (doesn't compete with action)

**Fall phase:** Minimal text (the visuals should carry this)
- If used: single-word or date, rarely full sentences
- Position: corner safe zone, small size

**Grind phase:** Minimal text (montage phase, visual momentum matters more than reading)
- Skip if possible
- If used: brief encouragement or time marker ("Months of training...")

**Turning point:** Optional emphasis text
- If used: a single word that captures the shift ("Finally" or "Ready")
- Size: medium, visible but not dominating
- Duration: 0.5–1 second

**Triumph:** The peak quote (if using text at all)
- Largest text size in the entire piece
- Most important line from any VO/speech
- 1–3 seconds hold
- Can appear mid-peak moment or after climax (depending on whether you want it to set up or emphasize the moment)

**Coda:** Credits or closing text
- Small-to-medium size
- 2–3 second hold
- Example: "© 2024" or "Follow @player" or a final quote

## Safe zones and mobile-specific placement

Text must be readable and not cut off on the smallest device (phone):

**Safe zone (all platforms):**
- Don't place text in outer 10–15% of screen edges (OS UI, notches, bezel cutouts)
- Never center text horizontally on mobile vertical (9:16) — it often gets cut by the phone's bezel; offset slightly left or right

**Mobile vertical (9:16, TikTok/Reels):**
- Text placed in bottom-third is safest (not competing with face-detail in upper-third)
- Avoid placing text over faces or fast-moving action (hard to read while movement is happening)
- Font size: minimum 24pt for body text, 48pt+ for titles
- Width: don't let text lines run the full width — narrow text column (70–80% of screen width) is easier to read quickly

**Widescreen (16:9, YouTube):**
- More flexibility for placement (face-safe at top, action-safe in middle)
- Text size can be slightly smaller (20pt body, 40pt+ titles)
- Width: full width is OK, but still easier to read if constrained slightly

## Typography basics (readability matters more than "cool")

**Font choice:**
- Sans-serif (Helvetica, Arial, modern variants) for social media — reads faster than serif on small screens
- Bold or semi-bold weight — thin fonts disappear on mobile
- Avoid script/handwriting fonts (they're almost never readable at small size, despite looking nice in preview)
- High contrast to background (white text on dark image, black text on light image — at minimum add a subtle drop shadow if you must use medium-contrast combinations)

**Size hierarchy:**
- Title/peak quote: 60–100+ pixels (depending on screen size)
- Secondary text (phase markers, subtle quotes): 30–48 pixels
- Credits/tiny metadata: 18–24 pixels
- Never go below 18pt on mobile

**Color:**
- White text with drop shadow is the safest (works over any background)
- Black text requires a bright/light background or a semi-transparent white box behind it
- Avoid thin, desaturated colors (light gray on medium-gray background = unreadable)
- Colored text (red, yellow) can work for emphasis, but confirm readable on every background it appears over

**Duration (how long text stays on screen):**
- Title card: 2–3 seconds (enough to read twice)
- Quote over b-roll: 3–4 seconds (viewers need time to read while watching action)
- Caption/metadata: 1–2 seconds minimum (could be longer if it's the only thing on screen)
- **Rule:** If a viewer needs to read the text, hold it longer than feels comfortable in the edit suite. Test on a phone.

## Common placements (template positions)

**Lower third (used for credits, speaker IDs, clarification):**
- Text positioned in bottom-third of frame, left-aligned or centered
- Common in sports broadcasts for player names, stats
- Lower-thirds often have a semi-transparent bar behind text (improves readability)

**Center-screen (used for titles, peak quotes, emphasis):**
- Dominates the screen, pulls full attention
- Use sparingly (2–3 times in a piece maximum)
- Usually holds longer (2–3+ seconds)

**Corner safe (used for metadata, credits, subtle direction):**
- Small text in corners (usually bottom-right or bottom-left)
- Doesn't interfere with main action
- Used for watermarks, channels, dates

**Overlay on b-roll (used when you want text to be present but secondary):**
- Smaller text overlaid directly on video
- Often a caption under speech/VO
- Requires strong contrast or drop shadow
- Don't use if action is happening in that screen region

## Text card types by use case

**Title card (function: clarification + emphasis)**
- Single scene (full black or color background, or video clip with text overlay)
- Appears at cold open
- 2–3 second hold minimum
- Example: "The Year Everything Changed" or just the name "Patrick Mahomes"

**Lower third (function: clarification)**
- Semi-transparent bar in bottom-third with text
- Tied to a specific clip (stays for duration of clip, then disappears)
- Use for: speaker names, locations, dates
- Example: "2015 - Injury Sidelines Star Player"

**Scroll/crawl (function: metadata)**
- Text moves across screen (scrolls left-to-right or up)
- Rarely used in social content (feels slow)
- Only use if metadata is substantial and you want it present but not dominating

**Animated entrance/exit (function: emphasis)**
- Text flies in or fades in, then flies out or fades out
- Don't overuse (1–2 animations per piece maximum)
- Useful for the peak quote (animates in as the moment hits)
- **Caution:** Keep animations fast and purposeful (0.3–0.5 sec entrance, not slow 1-second float)

**Static background (function: isolation)**
- Text placed on a solid color bar or semi-transparent box
- Guarantees readability regardless of background
- Use when overlaying text over very busy or very dark footage

## Text-to-audio sync (critical for speech-heavy content)

When placing a quote or speech excerpt on screen:

- Quote should appear **just before** the speaker starts saying it (give viewer a half-second lead)
- Quote should stay on screen for **entire duration** of the spoken phrase
- Quote should exit **just after** the phrase ends (don't leave it lingering after speech stops)
- For speed/emphasis: if the speech is fast, you can let the text stay slightly longer (gives the eye time to catch up)

**Misalignment feels amateur:** Text appears a full second after words are spoken, or disappears before the speaker finishes. Tight sync reads as intentional and professional.

## Genre-specific text conventions

**Football/sports:**
- Lower third with player name + number (very standard)
- Score or time (if not burned in the video itself)
- Stats during replay or highlight (sparingly)
- Avoid overloading with graphics — sports content already has logos/time/score from the broadcast

**Motivational/speech:**
- Peak line gets its own title card or large overlay (this is your emphasis moment)
- Speaker name/title in lower third (if the speaker isn't obvious)
- Don't caption every line of speech (that's overkill; caption only the crucial quote)

**Comeback/redemption:**
- Date/time markers for different era (2015 vs 2024)
- Single powerful quote near peak
- Avoid over-explaining ("The Fall," "The Grind") — let visuals do this, text only if absolutely needed

## Reasoning checklist for this stage

- [ ] Have I identified the ONE most important quote/text (the peak moment), and does it get the most prominent placement?
- [ ] Does every text card serve one of the three functions (clarification / emphasis / metadata)?
- [ ] Are text sizes readable on a phone at arm's length?
- [ ] Do text cards stay within safe zones (not cut by bezel/notch)?
- [ ] For mobile vertical, is text not in the center (offset for safer placement)?
- [ ] Do text cards have sufficient contrast (white on dark, or black on light, or drop shadow)?
- [ ] For speech/VO, does text sync tight with spoken words (appears before speech, exits after)?
- [ ] Am I using no more than 3–4 text cards in a 60-second piece (or proportionally fewer for shorter pieces)?
- [ ] Would I be able to understand this piece without any text, or does text do essential work?

## V5 addition — penalty scoreline template

For shootouts, use a two-line score treatment:

```text
TEAM_A 2–2 TEAM_B
TEAM_A wins 4–3 on penalties
```

Keep full-time score and shootout score visually distinct and away from broadcast scorebugs. Treat this as a typography template only, not a factual example. Replace team names/scores only after match facts are verified and logged with provenance.

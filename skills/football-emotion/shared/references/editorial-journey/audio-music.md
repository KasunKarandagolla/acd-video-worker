# Audio, Music, VO & SFX Editorial Reasoning

Read this during Stage 5 music/audio decisions and again during Stage 8 assembly and Stage 9 QA.

## Priority hierarchy

Before adding background music, decide which audio layer should dominate the section:

1. **Commentary / real speech** if the words carry meaning.
2. **Crowd / stadium sound** if collective emotion is the story.
3. **Silence** if tension, shock, or grief needs space.
4. **Narration / VO** if context is needed.
5. **Music** when it supports tone and pacing without stealing the moment.
6. **SFX** only to emphasize transitions or impacts sparingly.

## Music selection by emotion

- **Heartbreak / final chance lost:** sad piano, low ambient, silence, crowd-only.
- **Pressure / penalty / tunnel walk:** heartbeat tension, dark drone, sparse rise.
- **Comeback / proof:** cinematic rise, motivational build, restrained percussion.
- **Legacy / trophy / national pride:** orchestral lift after the story earns it.
- **Iconic skill:** rhythmic motivational music or light cinematic pulse.
- **Archive/documentary:** minimal documentary piano or low pad.

## Placement rules

- Do not lock music before brief interpretation is complete.
- For build-to-music assembly, place and mark music structure before final trim decisions.
- Duck music under speech by roughly 6-12 dB.
- Let silence or crowd replace music at the most delicate emotional moments.
- Avoid epic music throughout; emotional contrast is created by change, not constant intensity.

## SFX restraint

Use bass hits, whooshes, risers, shutters, and impact hits only when they clarify a cut or emotional shift. SFX cannot fix weak clip selection.

## License gate

No music or SFX asset can move from candidate to final audio plan until a `license_verification_record` marks it `approved`. Candidate catalogs are search aids, not legal approval.

# Brief Interpretation & Scope Locking

Read this FIRST, before any sourcing or curation begins. This stage converts a title/theme into specific story decisions that lock in everything downstream.

## The intake prompt: title → specific story

You'll receive something like: "Comeback football story" or "Motivational hustle edit" or "Player X rejection redemption." This is a topic, not a story yet. Your job is to generate a *specific* story hypothesis before moving forward.

The three questions that transform a topic into a story:

1. **"What's the one emotional question this piece answers?"** (see `narrative-structure.md` for this concept)
   - "Comeback" is generic. Specific: "Can someone written off as finished by their entire sport come back?"
   - "Motivational" is generic. Specific: "What does it cost, internally, to keep trying after public failure?"

2. **"What's the tonal flavor?"** (this gates music/FX choices downstream)
   - Triumphant? Bitter-sweet? Defiant? Introspective? Angry?
   - This is different from "high energy" — energy is pacing, tone is *how it feels*.

3. **"What's the runtime target, and what platform?"**
   - These are HARD constraints. A 15-second TikTok and a 3-minute YouTube piece tell fundamentally different stories from the same theme.
   - 15 sec = hook + payoff, drop setup and grind
   - 30 sec = hook + setup + brief grind + payoff
   - 60 sec+ = full arc (setup → fall → grind → turning point → triumph → coda)

## Locking in scope from runtime

Once runtime is set, it gates everything below. Use this rough allocation (adjust if needed, but don't violate dramatically):

### 15-second format (TikTok, Twitter/X)
- Cold open teaser: 1–2 sec
- Moment of payoff: 10–12 sec (often the same clip shown twice or from different angles)
- Coda/text card: 1–2 sec
- **Clip budget: 3–5 total clips**
- **Music:** Short-form loop, likely full-length; can't afford music build, use one sustained emotional note
- **Text:** Usually one key line, appear at peak
- **Structure type:** Single-moment focus, not arc

### 30-second format (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok)
- Cold open: 2–3 sec
- Setup/context: 3–4 sec
- Conflict/grind: 8–10 sec
- Payoff/triumph: 8–12 sec
- Coda: 1–2 sec
- **Clip budget: 8–12 clips**
- **Music:** Can build if a 30-sec drop is available; otherwise use loop starting at a strong point
- **Text:** Title + one key quote is reasonable
- **Structure type:** Simplified arc (setup → conflict → payoff), skip deep grind

### 60-second format (YouTube, longer-form social)
- Cold open: 2–3 sec
- Setup: 5–8 sec
- Fall: 8–12 sec
- Grind: 10–15 sec
- Turning point: 3–5 sec
- Triumph: 10–15 sec
- Coda: 3–5 sec
- **Clip budget: 15–25 clips**
- **Music:** Full structure (intro → build → drop → breakdown → outro)
- **Text:** Multiple title cards, quotes, chapter breaks are appropriate
- **Structure type:** Full Murch arc

### 3-minute+ format (YouTube long-form, documentary-style)
- All arc phases get real estate
- Music can be dynamic/multi-movement
- Text/graphics can be sophisticated
- Can afford digression and asides (interviews, slow moments, context-setting)
- **Clip budget: 30+ clips**
- **Structure type:** Can accommodate sub-arcs (a mini-comeback within the larger comeback)

## Platform-specific intake notes

These are not technical constraints (aspect ratio, loudness) — those come in Stage 11 (Platform Optimization). These are *editorial* constraints based on how each platform's audience consumes:

**TikTok / YouTube Shorts:** 
- Expect platform shift in 3 sec or less; if you haven't grabbed attention, it's over
- Vertical video (9:16) native — widescreen crops from different sources will look jarring
- Peak moment must be visually loud (not just audio)

**Instagram Reels:**
- Similar to TikTok but slightly older audience — can tolerate slightly longer cold opens (up to 5 sec)
- 1:1 square or 4:5 portrait format

**YouTube (10 sec – 10 min):**
- Can afford a longer cold open (up to 10 sec) because viewers clicked intentionally
- Widescreen (16:9) primary
- Audience expects more nuance, less frantic pacing than short-form

## Decision checklist before moving to sourcing

Before moving to Stage 2 (Sourcing), confirm you can answer all of these:

- [ ] What's the one emotional question this piece answers?
- [ ] What's the tonal flavor (triumphant/defiant/introspective/etc)?
- [ ] Runtime target (15s / 30s / 60s / 3min+)?
- [ ] Platform (TikTok / YouTube / Instagram / other)?
- [ ] From the runtime budget above, which arc phases get real estate?
- [ ] Can I articulate 2–3 specific "moments" or clips that *must* be in this piece (the non-negotiable shots)?
- [ ] If I can't yet, do I need more information about the subject to make those bets?

If you can't answer these clearly, don't proceed to sourcing — ask for clarification first. Sourcing with unclear scope is how you end up with hundreds of candidate clips and no ability to choose.

## How brief interpretation gates downstream decisions

- **Narrative structure** (which arc phases exist) ← runtime
- **Sourcing scope** (how many candidates to pull) ← runtime + tonal flavor
- **Music selection** (fast/slow, genre) ← tonal flavor
- **Clip count needed** ← runtime
- **Graphics & text intensity** ← platform + runtime
- **Color grading approach** ← tonal flavor (warm for triumph, cold for despair, etc.)

Don't lock music until brief intake is done. Don't start sourcing until brief intake is done. This stage is not overhead — it's the foundation.

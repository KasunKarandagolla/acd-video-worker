# Pacing, Rhythm & Cuts

Read this during Stage 4 sequencing, Stage 8 assembly, and whenever cut rhythm decisions are needed.

## Principle

Cut frequency should track emotional intensity, not stay constant. The edit should breathe during setup/fall, accelerate through grind, snap around the turning point, and either explode or hold silence at the payoff depending on the story.

## Phase pacing guide

- **Cold open:** fast clarity; 1-3 decisive shots, no slow explanation.
- **Setup:** moderate pace; hold enough for context.
- **Fall/pressure:** longer holds, faces, missed chances, tunnel walks, silence/crowd tension.
- **Grind/buildup:** progressively shorter cuts; training, attempts, commentary, crowd rises.
- **Turning point:** deliberate timing; one or two critical cuts should hit music/commentary precisely.
- **Triumph/climax:** fastest or loudest section, unless the emotion is heartbreak where a long hold may be stronger.
- **Coda:** slow release; avoid ending abruptly on the peak.

## Cut types

- **Hard cut:** default. Professional when timed well.
- **Beat cut:** useful in montage/skill/climax sections; do not cut on every beat mechanically.
- **Reaction cut:** after goal/miss/shock; reaction should land within 1-3 seconds of action.
- **J-cut/L-cut:** useful when clip length does not perfectly sync to music or commentary should lead the next image.
- **Dissolve/fade:** time jump, memory, archive, emotional cooldown. Overuse looks cheap.
- **Speed ramp:** only for meaningful action; 1-2 major uses per piece unless the style intentionally depends on it.

## Music sync

When music is locked, sync the 3-5 most important cuts only: turning point, major payoff, final emotional face, or biggest reveal. Do not over-engineer every minor cut.

## Mistakes

- Same cut pace through the whole video.
- Cutting away from emotional faces too quickly.
- Using transitions to hide weak structure.
- Keeping cool footage that does not serve the arc.
- Finalizing sync before the phase pacing is right.
